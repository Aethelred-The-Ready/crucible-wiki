# Armour

### Physical Armour
This armour is any which is manufactured and
created by the Smithing Skill. It is the most common
form of armour being made of toughened hide or
metal. It comes in a variety of forms and grants
Armour Points based on its material and the parts of
the body it covers. In order for armour to be
considered covering an area it must cover a total of
75% of the total area. The Armour Points granted by
coverage of each area can be found in the table
below. As an item, Physical Armour is a valid target
for the Destroy effect. If the armour is Indestructible, it
is instead reduced to zero(0) Armour Points, when
struck by a Destroy effect, and must be Refit.
Physical Armour is Refit by spending a sixty(60)-
second count role-playing adjusting straps, plates
and armour sections in order to ensure proper
coverage. It can also be Refit by the [Refit Physical
Armour](../glossary/refit#physical) effect, or the [Refit All Armour](../glossary/refit#all) effect.\
Should armour cover less than 75% of the area,
but at least 50% of the area the armour still grants
some protection, but it counts as one armour type
lower than normal. For example, a piece of Plate
armour which covers only the lower torso, such as a
gut plate, the armour counts instead as Mail. Leather
Armour counts as one(1) point less for all sections.

### Representations for Physical Armour
All Armour must be free of rigid protrusions which
may accidentally damage weapons, or harm other
players when struck. Additionally, all armour must
appear to be in period. Corrugated plastic sign
boards with no attempt to disguise their nature will
not be considered valid armour.\
As with weapons, all armour must be approved
prior to being used in game. This is to ensure that the
suit has the correct Armour Point value assigned and
it is free from potentially dangerous features.

### Leather
Leather Armour is represented by tough, but
flexible, materials such as leather, rope, suede, vinyl
and similar flexible materials as well as materials
which convincingly appear to be these materials.
Boiled Leather, and armour which has been
toughened in some way so as to be exceptionally
rigid instead counts as Mail. Exceptionally impressive
leather armour can also be counted as Mail.
Examples are; leather armour, leather scale, rope
armour and gambeson.

### Mail
Mail armour refers to a mesh of interlocking pieces
made of rigid materials which remain flexible due to
the way they are interconnected. This includes
chainmail as well as leather armours which
incorporate rigid materials over most of its area.
Examples are; Chainmail, ring armour, some forms of
splint mail.

### Plate
Plate Armour refers to armour which is composed
of rigid overlapping plates of armour as well as
materials which convincingly appear to be metal. The
armour must appear to be rigid in order to count as
Plate. Examples include; breastplate, full plate, scale
mail, brigandine and lamellar.
